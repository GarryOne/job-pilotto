# Pro plan: one-time payment, AI credit, top-ups (16 Oct 2026)

**Verdict:** Pro is a **one-time payment** (**$29, launch price $19**) that removes the application limit and includes **$5 of AI credit** on our in-house AI, enough for about one average job search. When the credit is gone our AI pauses until a top-up (own key stays unlimited). **After the free allowance, new work pauses until Pro, even with the person's own key.** Payments through Stripe Checkout; the site worker is the source of truth.
Owner (16 Oct 2026, via job-pilotto-cc): "a person needs to find a job once, not every month". Nothing here is built yet; nothing goes live until the owner switches Stripe to live mode.

## 1. Accounts
- A **Pro account** = an e-mail. Created by the Stripe payment (the e-mail Stripe collects), never by a password. D1 (`migrations/0079_pro.sql`):
  `pro_accounts(id, email_hash, email, created_at, status)`, `pro_devices(account, install, added_at, last_seen)`, `pro_purchases(id, account, kind, amount_cents (VAT included), currency, credit_micro, status, at, provider, provider_ref, country, tax_cents, fee_cents)` (adapter-neutral names: `provider`/`provider_ref` replace the Stripe ids; `country` = the buyer's ISO2 billing country, `tax_cents` = the VAT the tax service computed, `fee_cents` = the processor fee from the balance transaction, **null until known**, so `/admin/monetization` reads real net revenue and never guesses: net = amount − tax − fee), `pro_credit(account, delta_micro, reason, ref, at)` (append-only ledger; balance = sum, also kept as `pro_accounts.credit_micro` and changed only by an atomic `UPDATE … SET credit_micro = credit_micro - ? WHERE credit_micro >= 0`), `stripe_events(id)` (idempotency).
- **First device:** the app opens Checkout with `client_reference_id` = the install digest (`guard.js digestOf`), so the webhook links that install to the new account with no sign-in step.
- **Devices 2-3 and a new Mac:** "Add this device" in Settings → Plan: enter the e-mail, a 6-digit code is mailed, the install is added (max 3; remove one to add another).
  **Needs an e-mail sender (owner decision, below).** Until it exists, device 1 works fully and more devices wait.
- The server is the source of truth for Pro and the credit. The app asks `GET /api/pro/status` (install token, purpose `pro`) and **caches** the answer in settings with the time; if the site is unreachable the cache is trusted **14 days**, then the app behaves as Free (never silently locked mid-work). The offline `JP1.` key stays for founders/friends and as a second way to be unlimited, never the only check for paid Pro.

## 2. Payments: Stripe Checkout
- `POST /api/pro/checkout {kind: 'pro' | 'topup5' | 'topup10' | 'topup20'}` (install token) creates a Checkout Session (one-time `mode=payment`, price ids as Worker vars, `automatic_tax` on if the owner enables Stripe Tax, `consent_collection[terms_of_service]=required`, the withdrawal wording in `custom_text`) and returns its URL; the app opens it in the browser. No card data ever touches the app or our worker.
- `POST /api/stripe/webhook`: **signature verified** (`Stripe-Signature`, HMAC-SHA256 with `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, 5-minute tolerance, constant-time compare), event id stored in `stripe_events` (replay-safe). Handled: `checkout.session.completed` (+ `payment_status=paid`) → account, device, purchase, credit; `charge.refunded` / `charge.dispute.created` → §5. Everything else is acknowledged and ignored. No Stripe SDK: `fetch` to the API, so tests inject a fake.
- **Launch price:** one var `PRO_PRICE_ID` (the $19 price); the $29 price exists beside it; switching is a var change, not a deploy of code. The Upgrade screen reads the price and the "launch price" flag from `/api/pro/config`, so the app never hard-codes a number.
- **Top-up credit** (owner, 16 Oct: "~30%"; **the math below shows ~30% only holds where no VAT is inside the price**). **Decided (owner, 16 Oct): option B, prices include VAT, credits $2.80 / $5.80 / $11.80.** The table below is the check behind the old $3.25 / $6.75 / $13.75 proposal. Pro itself: the $5 credit is a **cap, not prepaid cost**; real spend is measured (§3).

  **Margin check** (margin = what is left of the price **without VAT** after Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30 (on the whole payment, VAT included) and the credit; Stripe Tax's ~0.5% and international-card/currency extras would lower every figure a little more):
  | Top-up | Stripe fee | Where | VAT | Price without VAT | After fee | Credit | Left | Margin |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | $5 | $0.45 | EU consumer | 20% | $4.17 | $3.72 | $3.25 | $0.47 | **11%** |
  | $5 | $0.45 | Switzerland, registered | 8.1% | $4.63 | $4.18 | $3.25 | $0.93 | **20%** |
  | $5 | $0.45 | No VAT | 0% | $5.00 | $4.55 | $3.25 | $1.30 | **26%** |
  | $10 | $0.59 | EU | 20% | $8.33 | $7.74 | $6.75 | $0.99 | **12%** |
  | $10 | $0.59 | CH | 8.1% | $9.25 | $8.66 | $6.75 | $1.91 | **21%** |
  | $10 | $0.59 | none | 0% | $10.00 | $9.41 | $6.75 | $2.66 | **27%** |
  | $20 | $0.88 | EU | 20% | $16.67 | $15.79 | $13.75 | $2.04 | **12%** |
  | $20 | $0.88 | CH | 8.1% | $18.50 | $17.62 | $13.75 | $3.87 | **21%** |
  | $20 | $0.88 | none | 0% | $20.00 | $19.12 | $13.75 | $5.37 | **27%** |
  EU rates differ by country (17-27%); 20% is the example. "CH 8.1%" applies only once the owner is VAT-registered in Switzerland (above CHF 100,000 turnover); before that, Swiss customers fall under "none".

  **Two ways to restore ~25% (owner chose B; A dropped because EU consumer law requires the shown B2C price to include VAT, so "+VAT at checkout" is not allowed for consumers):**
  - **A (dropped). Prices shown + VAT at checkout** (tax-exclusive; Stripe Tax adds it): the customer pays $5 + VAT, we always keep the $5. At 20% VAT: fee $0.47, left $5 − $0.47 − $3.25 = $1.28, **≈ 26%**. Credits stay $3.25 / $6.75 / $13.75. The shown price is not the final price in the EU (usual in the US, less so in the EU).
  - **B (chosen). Prices stay VAT-inclusive, credits sized for the worst case (EU 20%):** $5 → **$2.80**, $10 → **$5.80**, $20 → **$11.80** (≈ 25% in the EU; ≈ 35-40% where there is no VAT). The price on the button is the price paid, but the credit is smaller everywhere.
  Whichever is chosen, the Upgrade screen shows the credit it buys ("$5 buys …"), never hides the gap.

### Payment layer: one small interface (owner, 16 Oct: "Stripe for now… I can switch later")
`site/src/payments/index.js` exports `createCheckout(kind, install)`, `verifyEvent(request)` → `{id, type, account, amount, refunded...}` in **our** shape, and `refund(purchase)`. Only `payments/stripe.js` knows Stripe. The ledger, accounts, credit and the app never see a Stripe field. A merchant of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) later = one new adapter file plus a var; a contract test runs the same cases against every adapter.

### VAT and invoices in 5 plain lines (owner decides later; not legal advice, ask an accountant before live)
1. **What you register:** with Stripe you are the seller, so VAT is yours. As a **non-EU (Swiss) seller** of digital services to EU consumers, EU VAT is due **from the first sale**: the €10,000 threshold exists only for sellers established in the EU. You register once for the **non-Union OSS** in one EU country and file one quarterly return. (This is my reading of the EU rules; the accountant confirms before live.)
2. **Swiss VAT:** only once worldwide turnover passes **CHF 100,000** a year; other countries (UK, Norway, US states) have their own thresholds.
3. **What Stripe Tax does:** works out the right rate per customer and puts it on the receipt, about **0.5% per payment** on top of Stripe's fee; it does **not** register, file or pay for you. Proper VAT invoices need Stripe Invoicing on.
4. **Cost, rough:** a few hundred francs a year for an accountant for the OSS return (my estimate), plus Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30 and Tax's 0.5%. At 20% VAT, a $19 sale in the EU leaves about $15.80 before fees.
5. **The alternative:** a merchant of record carries all of this for about 5% + $0.50 per sale; the interface above makes that a later swap.

**Refunds / 14-day EU withdrawal:** for digital content the right ends once delivery starts with the buyer's express consent; Checkout collects it and the Terms say it. **Proposal:** refund Pro on request within 14 days, any reason; top-ups only for the unused part.

## 3. The AI credit ledger
- `free-ai.js` already computes `usd = costUsd(model, usage)` per call and writes `ai_calls`. For an install linked to a Pro account with credit, the same micro-USD is **debited from the account in the same `waitUntil` work** (atomic D1 update; one `pro_credit` row per call batch, `ref` = the ai_calls day/action). No second cost source.
- Per request, order of checks (code in a new `site/src/pro-ai.js`; `free-ai.js` calls it, grows by a few lines only):
  1. kill switch `FREE_AI=off` and the 50/min rate limit apply to everyone;
  2. Pro account with credit > 0 → **separate lane**: skip the per-install day/token/neuron caps **and** the global Free AI stop (owner: immune to it); no per-account daily guard.
  3. Credit warnings: the status carries `warn: 30 | 10 | 0`; the app shows them once each. **Credit at 0: no free fallback** (owner replaces "fall back to the Free limits"): the app starts no new work on our AI and says "top-up or own key"; **the application in progress finishes** (the route allows a small overdraft, **proposal $0.25**, so in-flight calls complete; beyond it the route refuses with the same message). Own key stays unlimited.
  4. No account (Free) → the Free limits, unchanged, only within the allowance (§3b).
- **Own AI key** never reaches our route, so nothing is debited and nothing counted, Pro or not.
- **The credit covers everything** the AI does (owner, 16 Oct): job reading and scoring, CVs, letters, applications, mail, interviews, insights. Debited from the same `ai_calls` micro-USD, whatever the action.
- **"AI credit: N% left"** = balance ÷ the credit ever added to the account (Pro's $5 + top-ups), rounded down to 5%. Pure arithmetic on the ledger, no estimate, no per-application cost needed. `/api/pro/status` returns `{creditPercent, creditMicro}`; the app shows the bar and the percent only.
- **What $5 buys** (cc's estimate for a whole ~3-month search on Free AI; **an estimate until `/admin/ai-cost` has measured per-task numbers**, then this table is replaced by them):
  | Search | AI cost | Credit |
  |---|---|---|
  | Light | ≈ $3.5 | $5 lasts, ~30% left |
  | Average | ≈ $5 | $5 is about one search |
  | Heavy | ≈ $7+ | needs a $5-$10 top-up |
  | Where it goes | job reading and scoring 60-70%; CVs, letters, applications, mail, interviews, insights the rest | |
  So $5 ≈ one average search; a heavy searcher tops up. The website says "AI credit for a whole job search; top up if you search heavily", no number of applications.
- Credit never expires (one-time payment; an expiry would read as a subscription).

### 3b. Free after the allowance: Pro is required, even with your own key (owner, 16 Oct)
"Once they cross the allowance, we need to force them in a way to pay us… otherwise, using the app with your own key, you can do that forever and you'll never feel the need to pay."
- **Free users:** Free AI works only **within the free allowance** (20 applications or 60 days, whichever lasts longer). After it, new work pauses **whatever the engine**, until Pro. Tracking, data, Notion and export keep working; an application in progress is never cut (the rule `app-allowance.js` already has).
- **Own key is enforced in the app only** (no server call for own-key AI), so the app checks the server for the plan: `GET /api/pro/status` returns `{plan: free|pro, credit…, license}`; the **licence check moves server-side** too (`JP1.` keys and the revoked list are verified by the site; the offline Ed25519 check stays as the cache/fallback). Offline grace: the last answer is trusted **14 days**, then the app behaves as Free (new work pauses only if the free allowance is over). A technical user can patch the app: accepted (FSL), as in `license.js` today.
- **Terms say it:** Free AI is for traction, never a permanent promise; Pro's $5 credit is the promise.

## 5. Abuse
| Case | Guard |
|---|---|
| One account spent across many installs | max **3 devices**; the install token is bound to **one** account; a 4th link is refused and flagged (`guard.flag`) |
| Fast drain (shared key, loop) | the 50/min limit (the only brake in the Pro lane, owner decision) + max 3 devices; the credit is the cap, a drained account just pauses at 0 |
| Refunded payment | `charge.refunded` → purchase `refunded`, credit of that purchase removed (balance floored at 0), Pro removed for a refunded Pro; the install returns to Free rules (§3b) |
| Chargeback | same as a refund, account marked `disputed`, no new top-ups |
| Webhook forged or replayed | signature + tolerance + `stripe_events` id |
| Shared e-mail/code | codes single-use, 10 minutes, 5 tries, rate-limited per IP and e-mail |
| Founder key shared | existing (`revoked` list), checked server-side too (§3b); Pro accounts do not use it |

- Tests: the Stripe API and clock are injected fakes; signed fixture events; D1 in memory (like `/admin` page tests); nothing reaches Stripe, KV production or the owner's app. A loop test covers **every** handled event kind and every cap message (class rule).
